01 · Start here

Know whether you need the GRE.

The GRE General Test is used by many graduate, business and some professional programmes. But requirements vary widely: some schools require it, others are test-optional, and some do not consider it at all.

🎓

What it measures

Verbal Reasoning, Quantitative Reasoning and Analytical Writing. It is designed to assess readiness for graduate-level study rather than subject-specific knowledge.

🔍

What to verify first

Check whether your programme accepts the GRE, whether it has minimum or average score expectations, and whether it weighs Analytical Writing or Quantitative Reasoning more heavily.

Do not assume: A university may be test-optional while a specific department still strongly prefers GRE scores. Use the official programme page and admissions office as the final source.
02 · Test format

Five sections. Under two hours.

03 · Scoring

Understand the score report.

You receive separate Verbal and Quantitative scores from 130–170, plus an Analytical Writing score from 0–6. There is no single combined GRE total.

MeasureScaleWhat it reflectsHow programmes use it
Verbal Reasoning130–170Reading, reasoning and vocabulary in contextOften important for humanities, social sciences and law-adjacent programmes
Quantitative Reasoning130–170Quantitative reasoning, algebra, geometry and data analysisOften important for business, engineering, economics and STEM programmes
Analytical Writing0–6Analytical writing and argument constructionMay receive more attention for writing-intensive programmes

ScoreSelect gives you control

With ETS ScoreSelect, you can choose which GRE scores from the last five years to send to designated institutions. This can reduce pressure if one test day does not go as planned.

Retakes are structured

You can take the GRE once every 21 days, up to five times in any rolling 12-month period. Plan retakes only after reviewing a detailed error log from your previous attempt.

Validity: GRE scores are valid for five years from the test date, but an individual programme may apply its own recency or submission policy.
04 · Setting a target

There is no universal “good GRE score.” A strong score depends on your field, degree level, the programme’s admitted-student profile and the weight it gives each measure.

05 · Preparation roadmap

An 8-week plan with a purpose.

Adapt the plan to your diagnostic result. The core loop is simple: practise timed questions, review every error, identify the cause, then drill that exact weakness.

WEEK 1 · DIAGNOSE

Find your baseline

Take a full-length official-style practice test. Record Verbal, Quant and Writing performance, timing losses and recurring error types.

WEEKS 2–3 · QUANT

Rebuild fundamentals

Review arithmetic, algebra, geometry, fractions, percentages, exponents and data interpretation. Practise Quantitative Comparison separately.

WEEKS 4–5 · VERBAL + WRITING

Develop reasoning

Learn vocabulary through sentences and roots, practise text completion and reading comprehension, and write timed issue essays.

WEEKS 6–7 · SIMULATE

Train under pressure

Take full timed mocks on a computer. Review pacing, careless errors and question-type patterns after every test.

WEEK 8 · REFINE

Polish and prepare

Do light review, revisit your error log, confirm booking details and protect sleep before test day.

06 · Section strategy

Small habits, better scores.

📖

Verbal: predict first

For text completion, predict the meaning of the blank before reading choices. For reading, identify the author’s claim, evidence and logical role of each paragraph.

🧮

Quant: choose the shortest path

Look for relationships, estimation and number properties before doing heavy calculation. In Quantitative Comparison, test strategic cases rather than solving fully.

✍️

Writing: analyse, don’t summarise

State a clear position, support it with reasons and examples, address counterarguments, and keep each paragraph focused on one idea.

⏱️

Pacing: bank easy points

Mark difficult questions, make a reasoned choice, and return only if time remains. Never leave a section with unanswered questions you could have attempted.

08 · Avoid these

Don’t lose points to avoidable errors.

01

Word lists without context

Learn vocabulary through sentences, roots and reading.

02

Ignoring Analytical Writing

Some programmes care about it more than applicants expect.

03

Overusing the calculator

It can slow you down when reasoning is faster.

04

No timed mocks

Pacing and stamina are separate skills.

05

Overstudying one section

Balance Verbal, Quant and Writing until the final weeks.

06

Booking too late

Leave room for preparation, score reporting and a retake.
