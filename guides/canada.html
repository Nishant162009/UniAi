Understand the Canadian education system

Start with the credential that matches your academic level and career plan.

Universities

Offer bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees. Program length and admission requirements vary by institution and field.

Colleges & polytechnics

Often emphasize applied learning, career-focused programs and, in some programs, work placements.

Vocational programs

Provide practical training for specific occupations and skills.

Language schools

English- and French-language programs can be used for language development or shorter study experiences.

Your Canada study roadmap

EduCanada's current journey is organized around finding a school, budgeting, applying to school, applying to study and coming to Canada.

1

Find a school & program

Match your interests, career goals, academic background and preferred location with eligible programs.

2

Build a realistic budget

Calculate tuition and living costs before applying. Keep funds available for the full study period and required expenses.

3

Apply to the institution

Prepare transcripts, language results and any program-specific documents. Follow the school's own deadline and document list.

4

Apply for a study permit

After receiving the required school documents, check the current IRCC requirements, including proof of funds and any PAL/TAL or Quebec requirements that apply to you.

5

Prepare for arrival

Arrange housing, travel documents, insurance/health coverage where applicable, banking, transportation and your first-week essentials.

Study costs in Canada

Costs vary significantly by program, institution, province and city. Use these figures as planning references, not guarantees.

University tuition

~CAD 41,746/year

EduCanada reports this 2026 average for international undergraduate university tuition.

Graduate tuition

~CAD 24,028/year

EduCanada reports this 2026 average for international graduate university tuition.

Living costs

CAD 23,000+/year

EduCanada advises budgeting at least this amount for living costs for one student, with more needed when family members accompany you.

Budget tip: Your real budget should include housing, food, transportation, health coverage, books, technology, phone/internet, winter clothing and an emergency reserve.

Study permit essentials

Immigration rules change. Always use the official Government of Canada / IRCC pages for the final requirements before submitting an application.

Core documents

  • Valid passport or travel document
  • Letter of acceptance from the institution
  • Proof of funds
  • Provincial/Territorial Attestation Letter (PAL/TAL) when required
  • Quebec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ) when studying in Quebec and required
  • Other documents requested by IRCC for your situation

2026 notes

  • Study permit processing fee: CAD 150.
  • Biometrics fee for an individual applicant: CAD 85 when biometrics are required.
  • From January 1, 2026, eligible master's and doctoral students at public DLIs are exempt from the PAL/TAL requirement.
  • PAL/TAL rules have other exemptions, so check the official eligibility tool for your case.

Important: verify before you pay

Use the official IRCC study-permit checklist and your institution's instructions. Do not rely on an old social-media post, agent message or outdated checklist when immigration requirements may have changed.

Working while studying

Work eligibility depends on your study situation and the conditions on your permit.

Off-campus work

Eligible students can generally work up to 24 hours per week during regular academic terms. During scheduled breaks, eligible students can work unlimited hours. Always confirm that you meet the current eligibility conditions.

Co-op & placements

As of April 1, 2026, post-secondary international students generally do not need a separate co-op work permit for student work placements such as co-op placements or internships, subject to the current rules.

Working is not a substitute for demonstrating the funds required for your study-permit application.

Preparing for arrival

Make your first week easier by handling the practical basics before you fly.

🏠

Housing

Compare residence, homestay and private rental options. Understand the deposit, lease, utilities and commute before committing.

💳

Money

Plan access to Canadian dollars, banking, cards and emergency funds. Keep important financial documents secure.

🧥

Weather

Canada's climate differs by region. Check the city-specific weather and prepare appropriate clothing for the season you arrive.

🚌

Transport

Research the local transit system, airport transfer and your route from housing to campus.

📚

Campus setup

Complete orientation, student ID, course registration and academic-system setup as instructed by your institution.

🤝

Support

Save your institution's international-student, academic advising, health and emergency contact information.
