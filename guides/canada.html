01 / Start here

Is Canada the right study destination for you?

Studying in Canada is not simply about choosing a famous university. Your decision can depend on your subject, academic profile, budget, province, city, programme structure, career plans and immigration circumstances.

Why students consider Canada

Canada has universities, colleges and other post-secondary institutions across a large and diverse country. Students can choose between major metropolitan campuses, smaller university cities and different academic pathways.

Canada is a federation. Education is primarily a provincial and territorial responsibility, so admission, tuition, academic structures and student services can differ between institutions and provinces.

Think beyond rankings

A university's overall reputation is only one part of the decision. Compare the exact programme, admission requirements, tuition, housing, location, co-op or placement opportunities, student support and career resources.

The right programme and total cost can matter more to your individual plan than a university's position in a general ranking.
02 / Why Canada

What makes Canada different?

There are several structural differences worth understanding before you compare Canadian institutions with other study destinations.

01

Wide geography

Students can choose from large metropolitan areas, smaller university towns and communities across multiple provinces and territories.

02

Research & innovation

Canada has research-intensive universities alongside institutions with strong professional and applied programmes.

03

Multicultural campuses

Many Canadian institutions have diverse student communities and international student populations.

04

Co-op pathways

Some programmes integrate co-operative education or work placements. Availability and eligibility depend on the programme.

05

Different province options

Tuition, housing, climate and day-to-day costs can differ substantially from one province and city to another.

06

English & French

Canada has two official languages, and some institutions and programmes operate in French, English or both.

12 / Study permit

The Canadian study-permit journey, step by step.

Immigration rules can change. Treat Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) and your institution's international office as the final authorities.

STEP 01

Receive your admission

Secure admission to an eligible Canadian institution and follow its instructions for international students.

STEP 02

Check your study-permit requirements

Review the current IRCC requirements that apply to your programme, country of residence and circumstances.

STEP 03

Prepare your documents

Gather the documents required for your application, including evidence related to admission and finances where applicable.

STEP 04

Submit the application

Complete the current IRCC application process and provide any identity, biometric or additional information requested.

STEP 05

Prepare for arrival

Once your immigration process is in place, organise travel, accommodation, documents and university enrolment plans.

13 / Accommodation

Where will you actually live?

Accommodation can become one of the biggest parts of your monthly budget, especially in major Canadian cities.

University residence

Campus or university-managed housing can be a convenient option, particularly for students new to the city.

Private student housing

Purpose-built student residences can offer furnished rooms and shared or private facilities.

Private rental

Renting a room or apartment privately can provide flexibility but requires careful attention to contracts, deposits, bills and location.

Before paying a deposit, understand exactly what is included, who manages the property, what the contract says, when the tenancy begins and which bills you are responsible for.
14 / Life in Canada

Studying is only half the experience.

Your day-to-day experience will depend on where you live, your university community, the season and how you manage your time and budget.

Public transport

Major cities have public transportation networks, while some smaller communities can be more car-dependent. Your costs depend on your city and commute.

Healthcare

Healthcare arrangements for international students vary by province and institution. Check the coverage applicable to your destination.

Student societies

Universities commonly offer clubs, societies, sports, volunteering and student support services.

Weather

Canada has major regional climate differences. Prepare for the conditions of your specific province and city rather than assuming one national climate.

Food & culture

Canada's diverse communities provide access to a wide range of cuisines and cultural events.

Student support

Many institutions provide international offices, academic advisers, career services and other student support resources.

15 / Work while studying

Understand your work conditions before taking a job.

Whether you can work and what conditions apply depends on your immigration status, programme and current Canadian rules.

Check your immigration conditions

Do not assume every international student has identical work rights. Review the current conditions attached to your status.

Keep academics first

Plan employment around your academic responsibilities and any restrictions attached to your immigration status.

17 / Avoid these mistakes

The problems usually start before the flight.

Mistake 01

Choosing an institution only because it appears high in a ranking without checking the exact programme.

Mistake 02

Calculating tuition but forgetting accommodation, transport, permit-related costs and everyday expenses.

Mistake 03

Waiting too long to understand programme requirements, application timelines or scholarship deadlines.

Mistake 04

Assuming every Canadian institution or province has the same admission process.

Mistake 05

Treating immigration requirements as something to solve only after receiving admission.

Mistake 06

Selecting a city without researching housing, commute time, climate and actual monthly living expenses.
