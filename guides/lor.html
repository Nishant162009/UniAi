01 · Start here

What is a Letter of Recommendation?

A Letter of Recommendation—also called a recommendation letter, reference letter or evaluation—is written by someone who has directly observed your academic, professional or personal work and can assess your potential.

🎓

What it adds

An LOR gives admissions committees an outside view of your ability, work ethic, curiosity, collaboration and readiness. It can add context that grades and test scores cannot.

📌

What it cannot fix

A recommendation cannot replace weak academic preparation or compensate for an unclear SOP. It works best when it confirms and expands on a coherent application story.

First rule: The university’s official requirements override general advice. Check how many letters are required, who may write them, whether they must be academic, and how they must be submitted.
02 · Choosing recommenders

Pick people who know your work.

A famous professor who barely knows you is usually weaker than a teacher, project guide or manager who can describe your contribution in detail. Prioritise depth of knowledge, relevance and credibility.

03 · Asking properly

Ask clearly, early and respectfully.

A good request makes it easy for your recommender to say yes—and easier to write a strong letter if they do.

✉️

Ask in person or by email

Explain the programme, deadline, submission method and why you are asking them specifically. Give them a genuine opportunity to decline.

📦

Send a recommender pack

Include your CV, SOP draft, transcript, project summaries, programme links, deadlines and the specific qualities you hope they can address.

Polite request template

Personalise every bracketed part. Do not send the same message without editing it.

Subject: Recommendation request for [Programme] — deadline [Date]

Dear Professor [Name],

I hope you are doing well. I am applying to [Programme] at [University], with a deadline of [Date]. Because you supervised my [project/course/work], I would be honoured if you would consider writing a letter of recommendation for my application.

Your perspective on my [specific skill/project/contribution] would be especially valuable because [one specific reason]. I have attached my CV, SOP draft, transcript and a short summary of the work we did together. The letter would need to be submitted through [portal/method] by [date].

I completely understand if your schedule does not allow it. Thank you for considering my request.

Sincerely,
[Your name]

04 · Recommender pack

Make their job easier.

A recommender pack is not a script. It is a concise set of facts and reminders that helps your recommender write accurately and specifically.

What to include

  • Updated CV or résumé
  • Final SOP or Personal Statement draft
  • Unofficial transcript
  • Programme links and deadlines
  • Submission portal instructions
  • A one-page summary of shared work
  • Two or three qualities you hope they can address
  • Specific results: grades, awards, metrics, outcomes

What not to include

  • A fully written letter presented as their own
  • Exaggerated claims or invented achievements
  • Twenty pages of unrelated certificates
  • Pressure to submit immediately
  • Requests to submit through your email account
  • Confidential letters shared with you
Shared-work summaryWeak versionUseful version
Project“I did a machine learning project.”“Built a crop-disease classifier using 4,000 images; improved validation accuracy from 78% to 89%.”
Role“I was a team member.”“Led a three-person team, divided tasks and presented the final prototype.”
Impact“It was successful.”“The dashboard reduced manual reporting time by about 40% during the pilot.”
Skill“I am good at coding.”“Implemented the data-cleaning pipeline and debugged deployment issues independently.”
Important: Recommenders must write and submit their own letters. Your pack should provide facts and context, not a finished letter for them to copy.
05 · Strong vs weak letters

What makes a letter persuasive?

Strong letters are specific, comparative and credible. They describe what you did, how you did it, what changed and why it matters for graduate study.

Weak[Name] is a hardworking and intelligent student. I highly recommend them for admission.
StrongerIn my Data Structures course, [Name] ranked among the top students and redesigned a inefficient search routine in the class project, reducing lookup time in the demo dataset. Their questions showed genuine interest in algorithmic trade-offs.
WeakThey are a good team player and have excellent communication skills.
StrongerWhen our team fell behind before the final review, [Name] reorganised the task list, coordinated with two members and presented the prototype clearly. The project was completed on time.
WeakI believe they will succeed in graduate school.
StrongerBased on their ability to identify a research gap, learn new methods quickly and revise their work after feedback, I believe they are well prepared for graduate-level research.

06 · Timeline

A realistic LOR schedule.

Start earlier than you think. Recommenders may be travelling, teaching, managing deadlines or reviewing many requests at once.

8–12 WEEKS BEFORE

Shortlist and research

Check programme requirements and identify people who know your work well.

6–8 WEEKS BEFORE

Send requests

Ask politely, share context and give recommenders a clear way to decline.

4–6 WEEKS BEFORE

Send the pack

Provide CV, SOP draft, transcript, project summary, links and deadlines.

2–3 WEEKS BEFORE

Check status

Confirm that each recommender has received the portal invitation.

7–10 DAYS BEFORE

Send one reminder

Be polite, brief and specific about the remaining deadline.

AFTER SUBMISSION

Thank them

Send a genuine thank-you note and update them on your result later.

07 · Myths vs truths

Stop believing these LOR myths.

MythTruthWhat to do instead
“A senior professor is always better.”A detailed letter from someone who knows you well is usually stronger.Choose knowledge and relevance over title alone.
“I should write the letter for them.”Recommenders must be the sole authors of their recommendations.Provide facts, reminders and context—not a finished letter.
“Confidential letters are risky.”Confidential letters are often viewed as more credible.Waive access where you are comfortable doing so.
“More letters are always better.”Extra letters may not be accepted or may dilute your application.Submit the required number unless the programme clearly welcomes extras.
“A family friend or relative can write one.”Family and close personal contacts are usually inappropriate.Choose academic, professional or verified mentor recommenders.
“A reminder is rude.”One polite reminder close to the deadline is professional.Send it 7–10 days before, with the portal link and deadline.
Undergraduate note: Requirements differ greatly. For example, Common App member colleges determine their own recommendation rules, and some require teacher evaluations while others do not.
