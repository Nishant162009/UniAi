01 · Start here

Money is the quiet part of studying abroad.

Why it matters

Tuition is only one cost. Rent, deposits, food, transport, insurance, phone and emergencies all arrive in a new currency, often before your first income.

Plan in layers

Start with fixed costs, add monthly living costs, then keep an emergency buffer. Rates, fees and rules change, so verify with official sources.

This guide gives principles, not exact figures. Check current numbers with your bank and your destination's official sites.

03 · Banking

Setting up your bank account.

Before you fly

Keep a card that works internationally, tell your bank your travel dates and carry only a small amount of local cash.

After you arrive

Open a student or standard account as soon as documents allow. You may need your passport, visa, admission letter and address proof.

Ask about monthly fees, international transfer charges and whether student accounts exist.

05 · Loans and funding

Education loans and sponsors.

Loans

Compare interest, repayment start, collateral and what happens if your plans change. Borrow only what you can realistically repay.

Sponsors

Keep clear records of who is funding you and in what amounts. Visa officers often check that your documents match.

06 · Working part-time

Earning while you study.

Check the rules

Work rights depend on your visa and destination. Always confirm hours and conditions on official immigration pages.

Keep study first

Part-time income can help, but it should not replace a solid funding plan.

Never count on future income to prove funds for a visa.
