01 · Start here

Know the test before you book it.

TOEFL iBT measures academic English across Reading, Listening, Speaking and Writing. Before paying, confirm the exact score scale, overall requirement, section minimums and deadline your university accepts.

🎓

Who usually needs it

Applicants whose prior education was not in English, especially for universities in the United States, Canada and other English-taught programmes. Some universities waive it based on prior study, nationality or other qualifications.

✅

What to verify first

Check whether the course accepts TOEFL iBT, whether it wants the 1–6 or 0–120 score, whether there are per-section minimums, and when the score must reach the institution.

Important: The updated TOEFL iBT launched worldwide on 21 January 2026. Older prep books, YouTube walkthroughs and mock tests may reflect the previous format.
02 · Test format

Four sections. Adaptive where it matters.

Reading and Listening use a multistage adaptive design: your performance can affect the difficulty, number of items and timing you see. The full test takes about two hours, excluding directions.

03 · Scoring

Two scales on one score report.

From 21 January 2026, TOEFL iBT reports four section scores and an overall score on a 1–6 scale in half-point increments. During a two-year transition period, reports also include a comparable overall score on the traditional 0–120 scale.

How the new overall works: ETS says the overall 1–6 score is the average of the four section scores, rounded to the nearest half band. For example, an average of 5.25 becomes 5.5.

05 · Preparation roadmap

A 6-week plan with a purpose.

Use a repeatable loop: practise under timed conditions, review every error, identify the cause, then target that weakness again.

WEEK 1 · DIAGNOSE

Find your baseline

Take an official-style diagnostic for the 2026 format. Record section performance, timing problems and recurring error types.

WEEKS 2–3 · BUILD

Fix foundations

Read academic articles daily, listen to lectures and discussions, build topic vocabulary and review grammar patterns.

WEEKS 4–5 · PRODUCE

Train Speaking and Writing

Record timed speaking responses and write timed academic responses. Review structure, relevance, accuracy and clarity.

WEEK 6 · SIMULATE

Polish and perform

Take full computer-based practice tests with a headset, review your error log, then protect sleep and logistics before test day.

07 · Module strategies

Small habits, better scores.

📖

Reading: prove every answer

Skim for structure, scan for names, dates and key terms, then read the exact supporting lines. Do not choose an answer just because it uses words from the passage.

🎧

Listening: predict, then verify

Use questions to predict the type of answer. Afterwards, check the transcript for synonyms, corrections, plurals and spelling details you missed.

✍️

Writing: answer the prompt

Plan your position and paragraph purpose first. Use specific support, keep the logic visible, and reserve time to check grammar and word choice.

🗣️

Speaking: develop, don’t memorise

Give a direct response, add a reason, then include an example or detail. Practise explaining unfamiliar topics naturally instead of reciting scripts.

09 · Avoid these

Don’t lose points to avoidable errors.

01

Using old materials

Practise the 2026 adaptive format, not pre-2026 question types.

02

Ignoring the scale

Check whether your school wants 1–6, 0–120, or both.

03

Skipping headset practice

Listening and Speaking depend on audio comfort and clear recording.

04

No timed practice

Adaptive sections reward consistent pacing, not slow perfectionism.

05

Forgetting section minimums

An overall score alone may not satisfy the requirement.

06

Booking too late

Leave time for score reporting before application deadlines.
