System

Two kinds of university.

Research universities (WO)

Academic, research-oriented. Bachelor's programmes usually last 3 years, followed by a 1–2 year master's.

Applied sciences (HBO)

Practice-oriented, with internships. Bachelor's programmes usually last 4 years.

Language

Many programmes are taught in English. Policy on English-taught programmes is evolving, so check each one.

Credits

Programmes use ECTS credits, with 60 credits per year.

Cities

Pick a city and campus.

Amsterdam

Capital with major universities and a large international community.

Delft and Eindhoven

Leading technical universities in engineering and design.

Utrecht and Leiden

Strong research universities in historic cities.

Groningen, Wageningen, Maastricht

Smaller student cities with distinct specialities.

Admissions

How to apply.

Rules differ by programme and level.

Step 1

Choose programmes

Compare content, language, entry requirements and deadlines on official pages.

Step 2

Register in Studielink

Most applications go through Studielink, plus the university's own portal.

Step 3

Submit documents

Transcripts, language proof and any programme-specific materials.

Step 4

Meet deadlines

Non-EU deadlines are often early in the year, and limited-seat programmes can close in January.

Costs

Budget the whole year.

Figures change yearly; check official fee pages.

Tuition

Non-EU students usually pay institutional fees that vary widely by university and programme.

Living costs

Rent is the biggest expense, especially in Amsterdam, Utrecht and Delft.

Insurance

Check which health insurance applies to you.

Other costs

Residence permit costs, bike, books and transport.

Do not rely on one budget estimate. Use each university's official fee and living-cost pages.
Funding

Look for funding early.

Holland Scholarship

Offered to some non-EEA students. Check current availability.

University scholarships

Many universities offer merit awards with early deadlines.

Erasmus Mundus

Joint master's programmes with funding for selected students.

Other funding

Foundations and external awards may apply.

Visa

Permits and arrival.

Immigration rules change. Use the IND and your university as the authority.

Step 1

Admission

You need an offer from a recognised institution.

Step 2

University applies

The university usually applies for your residence permit through the IND.

Step 3

MVV and permit

Many non-EU students collect an MVV entry visa, then a residence permit.

Step 4

Arrive and register

Complete municipal registration and other required steps.

Housing

Start early.

Shortage

Student housing is scarce in major cities, so start searching as soon as you are admitted.

University housing

Some universities offer rooms for international students, often with application deadlines.

Private rooms

Check contracts, deposits and registration rules, and beware of scams.

Commute

Cycling is central to Dutch student life.

Work

Working while you study.

Permission

Non-EU students usually need employer permission to work and have limits on hours. Check current IND rules.

After graduation

An orientation year permit may let graduates stay to look for work. Check eligibility.

Dutch language

Helpful for jobs and daily life even with an English-taught degree.

Priority

Keep study first, as your permit depends on progress.

Avoid these

Common mistakes

Applying late

Deadlines and housing both close early.

Ignoring housing

Many students arrive without a room.

Not checking fees

Fees differ widely by university.

Assuming work rules

Check your permit conditions before taking a job.
