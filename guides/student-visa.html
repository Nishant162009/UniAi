01 · Start here

What is a student visa?

The basics

A student visa (or study permit) is permission to enter and study in a country. Every country has its own rules, forms, fees and checks.

Start with the offer

You usually cannot apply until a university gives you an offer or enrolment confirmation. Some countries need extra documents: CAS (UK), COE (Australia), I-20 (USA) or an acceptance letter (Canada).

Visa rules change often. Treat this guide as a map and the official government website as the final authority.
02 · The process

Eight steps from offer to approval.

STEP 1

Receive your offer

Accept it and pay any deposit the university requires.

STEP 2

Read the official visa page

Find the exact form, fee and documents for your nationality.

STEP 3

Arrange funds

Prepare bank statements, loan letters or scholarship proof as required.

STEP 4

Collect documents

Passport, academic records, test scores, photos and the offer letter.

STEP 5

Health and biometrics

Some countries need medical tests, insurance or biometric appointments.

STEP 6

Submit and pay

Apply online or via the visa centre and keep every receipt.

STEP 7

Interview if asked

Be ready to explain your course, university, funding and plans.

STEP 8

Track and travel

Avoid non-refundable travel before approval unless you accept the risk.

06 · Funds

How to show you can afford it.

What officers want

Clear evidence of tuition plus living costs. Money should be traceable, available when required and consistent with your story.

Common sources

Savings, parents or sponsors, education loans, scholarships and blocked or guaranteed accounts, depending on the country.

Never use borrowed, fake or temporarily deposited funds. It can lead to refusal or bans.
07 · Interview

Common interview questions.

Q1

Why this course?

Link it to your background and career goal.

Q2

Why this university?

Name programme features, not only rankings.

Q3

Who pays?

Know the sources, amounts and relationships.

Q4

What after graduation?

Give a realistic plan, not vague answers.

Q5

Why not study at home?

Be honest about what the course offers.

Q6

Where will you live?

Show you have researched costs and options.

08 · Refusals

Why visas get refused.

01

Weak funding proof

Gaps, unexplained deposits or missing sponsors.

02

Inconsistent story

Answers that do not match documents.

03

Unclear study purpose

Course not linked to background or goals.

04

Incomplete forms

Missing signatures, photos or items.

05

False information

Can lead to serious long-term consequences.

06

Late applications

Processing times vary, so start early.
