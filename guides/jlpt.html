01 · Start here

What is the JLPT?

The test

The Japanese-Language Proficiency Test measures Japanese as a non-native speaker. It has five levels, from N5 (basic) to N1 (advanced).

Why take it

Many Japanese universities, employers and visas use it as proof of ability. Courses taught in Japanese often ask for N2 or N1.

Check the level your university or visa route requires.

03 · Format

Sections and scoring.

Sections

Language knowledge (vocabulary and grammar), reading and listening. At lower levels some parts are combined.

Scoring

Results use scaled scores with section minimums, so you must clear both the total and each section. Confirm details on the official JLPT site.

04 · Study roadmap

How to prepare.

PHASE 1

Foundations

Master hiragana and katakana, then core grammar.

PHASE 2

Kanji and vocabulary

Learn kanji in context with spaced repetition.

PHASE 3

Reading and listening

Read graded texts and listen daily.

PHASE 4

Mock exams

Practise full timed papers and review errors.

05 · Booking

Test dates and registration.

When

The JLPT is usually held twice a year, in July and December, though some locations offer one sitting. Check your country's centre.

Register early

Registration windows are short and centres can fill. Check the official site and your local organiser.

Bring your admit card and ID on test day.
