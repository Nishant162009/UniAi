01 · Start here

Know the JLPT before you register.

The Japanese-Language Proficiency Test measures Japanese ability for non-native speakers. It does not test speaking or productive writing; it focuses on language knowledge, reading and listening.

🇯🇵

What it is for

Universities, employers, language schools and some visa or professional pathways may use JLPT results as evidence of Japanese ability. Japanese-taught programmes often look for N2 or N1.

🎯

Choose the right level

Do not choose a level just because it sounds impressive. Match it to your current ability, your goal, and the requirement set by your school, employer or programme.

Important: JLPT is offered twice a year in July and December, but some overseas cities offer only one administration. Always confirm dates, fees and registration steps with your local host institution.
02 · Levels

03 · Test format

Sections differ by level.

N1 and N2 have two test sections: Language Knowledge (Vocabulary/Grammar) and Reading combined, followed by Listening. N3, N4 and N5 have three test sections, with Vocabulary separated from Grammar and Reading.

04 · Scoring

180 points, but passing is not just 180.

Every level uses a total scaled-score range of 0–180. To pass, you must meet the overall pass mark and also reach the minimum score in every scoring section.

05 · Preparation roadmap

A repeatable study system.

The right timeline depends on your starting point, target level and weekly study hours. Use this as a structure, then adapt it after your first diagnostic mock.

PHASE 1 · FOUNDATIONS

Master kana and core patterns

Learn hiragana and katakana until recognition is automatic. Build basic sentence patterns, particles and everyday vocabulary.

PHASE 2 · INPUT

Grow kanji and vocabulary

Learn kanji through words and sentences, not isolated characters. Use spaced repetition and review readings, meanings and common compounds.

PHASE 3 · SKILLS

Train reading and listening

Read graded texts daily and listen to Japanese every day. After practice, check transcripts, unknown words and grammar you missed.

PHASE 4 · EXAM MODE

Simulate and refine

Take full timed mock papers, maintain an error log, and repeat the exact question types causing the most losses.

06 · Kanji and grammar

Learn Japanese in context.

Kanji is not a separate subject from vocabulary. The most efficient approach is to learn characters through high-frequency words, example sentences and repeated reading.

AreaWhat to practiseCommon trapBetter habit
KanaInstant recognition, stroke order and reading fluencyConfusing similar charactersRead short passages aloud and drill minimal pairs
KanjiReadings, meanings, radicals and compoundsMemorising isolated charactersLearn words such as 学生, 学校 and 大学 together
VocabularyMeaning, collocations, pitch accent and usageTranslating word-for-wordLearn whole phrases and example sentences
GrammarParticles, verb forms, connectors and nuanceCollecting rules without using themMake your own example sentences and get feedback
ListeningPrediction, numbers, names and key transitionsTrying to understand every wordListen for structure, then verify with transcripts
Spaced repetition: Review new items after one day, three days, one week and then at increasing intervals. Re-test yourself from Japanese to English and from English to Japanese.
07 · Booking and test day

Registration windows are short.

The first test is generally in July and the second in December. Overseas application periods are commonly around March–April for July and August–September for December, but they vary by city and local host institution.

08 · Avoid these

Don’t waste an exam sitting.

01

Skipping kana

Weak hiragana or katakana slows every later skill.

02

Ignoring listening

Listening has its own pass requirement.

03

Isolated kanji drilling

Learn kanji through vocabulary and sentences.

04

Choosing the wrong level

Use a diagnostic mock before registering.

05

No timed practice

Reading and listening are speed-sensitive.

06

Missing registration

Local windows and seats can close early.
