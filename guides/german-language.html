01 · Start here

Do you need German?

It depends on your programme. English-taught degrees may not require German for admission, but German-taught programmes usually require B2 or C1—and daily life, part-time work and integration become much easier with German.

🇩🇪

For university

German-taught bachelor’s and master’s programmes often require B2 or C1. English-taught programmes may require IELTS or TOEFL instead, but still benefit from German for everyday life.

🎯

Know your target

Check the exact CEFR level, accepted certificate, required score or TDN result, validity expectations and whether the university accepts online or in-person testing.

Do not assume: “German-taught” and “English-taught” programmes can have different language rules. Always use the official course page and university admissions office as the final source.
02 · CEFR levels

A1 to C2 at a glance.

The Common European Framework of Reference describes what you can do with the language—not just how many words you know. Each level builds on vocabulary, grammar, listening speed and communication confidence.

LevelPlain-language abilityTypical useMain challenge
A1Basic introductions, simple questions and everyday phrasesSurvival GermanArticles, pronunciation and basic sentence structure
A2Simple exchanges about routine topicsEveryday basicsPast tenses, separable verbs and cases
B1Independent communication on familiar topicsWork, travel and everyday studyConnectors, subordinate clauses and more vocabulary
B2Clear, detailed discussion and understanding of complex textsOften accepted for German-taught studyPassive voice, formal writing and listening speed
C1Flexible, effective use in academic and professional contextsCommon for university entryNuance, academic vocabulary and extended speaking
C2Near-native command and precise expressionRarely requiredIdiomatic precision and stylistic control
Level rule: Do not choose an exam level based on how long you have studied. Take a placement test or mock exam, then target the level your university actually requires.
03 · German exams

Choose the exam your university accepts.

Goethe, TestDaF, DSH and telc all assess German, but they differ in level coverage, format, availability and typical use. The right exam is the one your university explicitly accepts.

ExamLevel coverageCommonly used forKey differenceBefore booking
Goethe-ZertifikatA1–C2General, academic and professional proofSeparate exams by CEFR level; widely recognisedConfirm the exact level and exam format
TestDaFB2–C1Admission to German-taught universitiesAcademic orientation; TDN 3–5 results per skillCheck the TDN result your course requires
DSHMainly university-entry levelGerman university admissionUsually organised or recognised by German universitiesConfirm whether your university accepts your DSH result
telc DeutschA1–C2General, work and academic purposesBroad exam portfolio, including specialised versionsConfirm the exact telc exam and level
🏫

Goethe-Zertifikat

A strong general-purpose option because Goethe offers exams from A1 to C2. It is useful for university, professional recognition and personal milestones, but always confirm that your specific programme accepts the exact level.

🎓

TestDaF and DSH

TestDaF is explicitly positioned at B2–C1 and is commonly used for German-taught university admission. DSH is another university-focused route, but acceptance and administration can vary by institution.

Exam-selection rule: If your university names “TestDaF 4 in all sections” or “Goethe C2,” treat that exact wording as the requirement—not a general equivalent.
04 · TestDaF focus

Four skills. Three TDN levels.

TestDaF assesses Reading, Listening, Writing and Speaking separately. Results are reported as TDN 3, TDN 4 or TDN 5 for each skill; many German-taught programmes ask for TDN 4 in every section.

SkillStructureWhat you doStrategy focus
Reading3 texts, 30 items, 60 minutesUnderstand academic and study-related textsSkim for structure, scan for evidence, manage time
Listening3 audio texts, 25 items, 40 minutesFollow interviews, discussions and presentationsPredict answers, note key transitions and numbers
Writing1 guided taskUse data and prompts to build an argumentDescribe trends, compare data, state a clear position
Speaking7 tasks, about 35 minutesHandle university-style situations and discussionsUse reusable structures, speak clearly and keep moving
TDN resultPlain-language orientationTypical university use
TDN 5Very strong academic GermanMay meet demanding programme requirements
TDN 4Strong academic German with minor limitationsCommon requirement for German-taught degrees
TDN 3Satisfactory but more limited academic GermanMay be insufficient for many direct-entry programmes
Important: TestDaF results are calculated separately for each skill. A high Reading result does not compensate for a lower Speaking or Writing result if your programme requires TDN 4 in all parts.
05 · Study roadmap

A realistic routine that compounds.

Language learning rewards frequency. Short daily sessions, weekly speaking and monthly mock testing will usually beat irregular cramming.

DAILY · 30–60 MIN

Build the base

Review vocabulary with spaced repetition, read a short text, listen to German and write a few sentences using new grammar.

WEEKLY · SPEAK

Use the language

Practise with a tutor, language partner or speaking group. Record yourself and review pronunciation, pauses and repeated errors.

MONTHLY · TEST

Measure progress

Take a mock exam at your target level. Track Reading, Listening, Writing and Speaking separately.

FINAL MONTH · SIMULATE

Exam mode

Do full timed practice, review every error, and rehearse the exact task formats used by your chosen exam.

Consistency matters more than intensity. Even 30 focused minutes daily compounds quickly.

06 · Grammar and vocabulary

Learn patterns, not isolated rules.

German grammar becomes manageable when you learn articles, cases and word order through complete phrases. Avoid collecting rules you never use in speaking or writing.

AreaWhat to practiseCommon trapBetter habit
Articlesder / die / das with every new nounLearning nouns without genderLearn “das Buch,” “die Wohnung,” “der Termin” as fixed chunks
CasesNominative, accusative, dative and genitiveUsing the wrong article after prepositionsLearn common preposition + case combinations
VerbsPresent, perfect, simple past, modal and separable verbsTranslating English tense patterns directlyPractise complete sentences and everyday situations
Word orderMain clauses, subordinate clauses and verb positionKeeping English word orderRead aloud and copy model sentences
VocabularyThemes, collocations and academic languageMemorising isolated translationsLearn words in example sentences and topic sets
Spaced repetition: Review new words after one day, three days, one week and then at increasing intervals. Always test yourself from German to English and English to German.

09 · FAQ

Quick answers.

There is no fixed timeline. It depends on your starting point, study intensity, language background and exposure. Use a placement test rather than copying someone else’s schedule.
German grammar can be demanding, especially articles, cases and word order. But it is highly pattern-based, so consistent practice and exposure make steady progress possible.
Some programmes are taught entirely in English and may not require German for admission. However, German remains valuable for daily life, part-time work and wider integration.
The best exam is the one your university or authority explicitly accepts at the required level. For German-taught degrees, TestDaF and DSH are common university-focused options.
TDN 4 is a TestDaF result level indicating strong academic German. Many German-taught programmes require TDN 4 in Reading, Listening, Writing and Speaking.
Use the official Goethe-Institut, TestDaF, DSH or telc websites for exam details, and your university’s official course page for admission requirements.
