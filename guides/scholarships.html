01 · Start here

What is a scholarship, really?

The idea

A scholarship is funding that does not need repayment, given for merit, need, subject, background, research potential or a mix. It may cover tuition, living costs, travel or only part of them.

Why start early

Many awards close months before admission deadlines, and some need a separate application, references and essays. Starting 6 to 12 months ahead gives you real options.

Funding changes every cycle. Verify amounts, eligibility and deadlines on the provider's official page.

03 · Truth vs myth

What is true and what is not.

MythOnly toppers get scholarships.
TruthGrades help, but many awards also weigh need, field, background, leadership or research fit. Competition is still strong.
MythFull scholarships are easy to find.
TruthMost awards are partial. Full funding is rare and very competitive.
MythAn agent can guarantee you a scholarship.
TruthNobody can guarantee one. Be careful with anyone who promises results or asks for payment to secure funding.
MythA high IELTS or GRE score earns a scholarship.
TruthScores help you meet eligibility. They rarely win funding on their own.
MythSmall scholarships are not worth the effort.
TruthSmall awards add up and often have fewer applicants.
MythYou apply for scholarships after getting admission.
TruthSome do require an offer, but many deadlines come earlier or need a parallel application.
MythOne strong application is enough.
TruthApply to a spread of options and track each one carefully.
MythCountries with low tuition need no funding.
TruthLiving costs, insurance and travel still matter. Scholarships can cover these.
MythThe same essay works everywhere.
TruthReaders notice generic essays. Tailor each to the award and its purpose.
MythIf you are rejected once, you have no chance.
TruthMany scholarships allow reapplication in a later cycle. Ask for feedback where possible.

07 · Essays

What selectors like to see.

Strong signals

Clear goals, evidence of impact, leadership or persistence, and a believable plan for how the funding helps your plans and your community.

Weak signals

Generic praise, repeated CV lines, exaggeration and answers that ignore the question or the scheme's mission.

09 · Scam alert

How to spot fake offers.

01

Paying to "secure" a scholarship

Real providers do not sell awards.

02

Guaranteed funding

No honest provider guarantees success.

03

Pressure to act now

Scammers use urgency to rush you.

04

Unofficial email or website

Check the web address and contact details independently.

05

Asking for bank passwords

Never share them.

06

Too good to be true

Verify on the official provider site.
