01 / Start here

Is Japan the right study destination for you?

Studying in Japan is about more than choosing a famous university. Your decision should match your subject, language ability, academic profile, budget, preferred city, course language, career goals and immigration plans.

Why students consider Japan

Japan combines a major higher-education system with strong research, engineering, technology, design, business and cultural opportunities. The country also has universities offering degree programmes taught in English.

The best choice depends on the exact programme, not only the university name.

Think beyond rankings

Compare course content, language of instruction, entrance requirements, tuition, scholarships, location, accommodation, internships, career support and the environment in which you will actually live.

Japan has national, public and private universities, plus other specialist institutions.
02 / Education system

Understand the Japanese higher-education system.

Japan has several higher-education routes. Choose based on your academic level, intended career and preferred learning format.

Undergraduate university

University bachelor's programmes generally take four years. Medical, dental, veterinary and some pharmaceutical programmes generally take six years.

Graduate school

Master's and doctoral study is available across many fields. Research-focused preparation and supervisor requirements can be especially important for graduate applicants.

Specialized training colleges

Professional and vocational routes can suit students seeking practical training in specific occupations and fields.

Japanese language institutes

Language schools can be part of a longer study pathway, especially for students who need additional Japanese-language preparation before further study.

03 / Explore Japan

Japan is more than Tokyo.

Japan has eight major regions with distinct cultures, environments and city styles. Location can change your daily experience as much as your university choice.

Kanto

Tokyo & surroundings

Dense metropolitan life, major universities, extensive transport and a large concentration of companies and industries.

Kansai

Osaka • Kyoto • Kobe

A major student region combining large cities, historic Kyoto, research institutions, business and cultural life.

Chubu

Nagoya & central Japan

A strong manufacturing and engineering environment with access to central Japan's cities and mountains.

Tohoku

Sendai & the north-east

University communities, seasonal landscapes and a quieter alternative to the largest metropolitan areas.

Kyushu

Fukuoka & Kyushu

Major universities, a lively regional hub and a location with strong links across East Asia.

Hokkaido

Sapporo & the north

Large open spaces, a distinct climate and a major university city with a different pace of life.

07 / Admissions

Japan does not have one universal application route.

Requirements vary by institution and programme. International applicants may encounter school-specific entrance exams, document screening, interviews, EJU and language tests.

Academic documents

Expect documents such as graduation certificates, transcripts and other school-specific application materials.

Language proof

Depending on the programme, you may need Japanese or English proficiency evidence. Check the exact programme rather than assuming one test is required everywhere.

EJU

The Examination for Japanese University Admission for International Students is used by many institutions as part of international undergraduate admissions.

Interview / entrance exam

Some institutions assess applicants through interviews, subject examinations, essays, application review or combinations of these.

09 / Costs

Budget for the whole experience, not just tuition.

Costs vary by institution, city and lifestyle. Use official university fee pages and current living-cost guidance for your exact plan.

10 / Scholarships

Look at funding before you finalise your shortlist.

MEXT

The Japanese Government (MEXT) Scholarship supports eligible international students through different scholarship categories. Requirements and application routes vary.

JASSO

JASSO offers scholarship support and other programmes for eligible international students. Availability and conditions vary by programme and institution.

University scholarships

Universities may offer tuition reductions, exemptions or scholarships based on academic performance and other criteria.

External funding

Foundations, local governments and other organisations may provide scholarships for particular nationalities, fields or circumstances.

11 / Student visa

Understand the COE → visa → arrival process.

Immigration rules can change. Use the Japanese government's current immigration guidance and your school's international office as the final authority.

STEP 01

Get accepted

You first need admission to a qualifying educational institution in Japan.

STEP 02

Certificate of Eligibility (COE)

In many cases, the accepting school acts as your proxy to apply for the COE through the regional immigration services bureau.

STEP 03

Visa application

After the COE is issued, apply through the Japanese embassy or consulate in your country or region of residence, following the current document requirements.

STEP 04

Enter Japan

Carry the required travel and immigration documents and complete the relevant procedures after arrival.

12 / Accommodation

Your room is part of your study plan.

University dormitory

Often useful for first-year international students who want a structured environment and easier access to campus.

Private apartment

Can offer more independence, but check deposits, guarantor requirements, contract terms, utilities and commute time.

Share house

A possible option for students who want a more social living environment and may prefer simpler move-in arrangements.

What to compare

Rent, initial fees, furniture, utilities, internet, transport, distance to campus and cancellation terms.

13 / Life in Japan

Studying is only half the experience.

Your daily life will depend on your city, university community, Japanese ability, budget and how you organise your time.

🚆 Transport

Japan has extensive rail and public-transport networks, especially in major cities. Compare commute time as well as rent.

🍱 Food

Convenience stores, supermarkets, university dining and restaurants provide many options, with major cities offering especially broad international choices.

🌸 Seasons

Japan has pronounced seasonal variation. Prepare for the climate of your specific region rather than planning around Tokyo alone.

🗣 Japanese

Even when your degree is taught in English, Japanese can make everyday tasks, friendships and part-time opportunities easier.

🏫 Campus life

Student clubs, societies, international offices and campus events can become a major part of your experience.

🤝 Community

Look for international-student support, language exchanges and local community programmes to build a wider network.

14 / Work while studying

Check your immigration conditions before working.

Permission matters

Do not assume a Student status automatically permits every type of employment. Check the current immigration conditions and required permission.

Keep study first

Plan any permitted work around your academic schedule and make sure it does not interfere with your studies.

16 / Avoid these mistakes

The problems usually start before the flight.

Mistake 01

Choosing a university only because it is famous without checking the exact programme.

Mistake 02

Assuming an English-taught programme means Japanese will never matter in daily life.

Mistake 03

Ignoring school-specific entrance exams, EJU or language requirements until late.

Mistake 04

Budgeting only for tuition and forgetting accommodation, transport and setup costs.

Mistake 05

Leaving the COE and visa process until the last minute.

Mistake 06

Choosing a city without researching commute, climate and actual living conditions.
