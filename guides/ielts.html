01 · Start here

Know the test before you book it.

IELTS measures your ability to use English in listening, reading, writing and speaking. Your first job is to confirm which test and score your university, employer or visa route actually requires.

What IELTS is for

Universities, professional bodies, employers and immigration authorities may use IELTS results as evidence of English proficiency. Acceptance and minimum scores depend on the specific institution or route.

What to verify first

Check the exact test version, overall band, minimum score for each module, result deadline, and whether an alternative English qualification or exemption is accepted.

Important: The country ranges in this guide are planning examples only. Always verify the current requirement on the official course, institution and visa pages before paying for a test.
02 · Test format

Four modules. Four different strategies.

The overall score is based on Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. Build a separate practice routine for each module instead of preparing for “English” in a vague way.

04 · Band scores

Understand the band scale.

IELTS uses bands from 0 to 9. The descriptors below are simplified orientation—not a substitute for the official band descriptors.

BandCommon labelPlain-language guide
9ExpertFull operational command of English, with very high accuracy and appropriateness.
8Very goodVery strong command; occasional inaccuracies or misunderstandings may occur.
7GoodHandles complex language and detailed reasoning well, though some errors remain.
6CompetentGenerally effective use despite errors and some misunderstanding.
5ModestPartial command; can manage familiar communication but often struggles with complexity.
4 and belowLimited to very limitedMore restricted ability; performance depends heavily on familiar situations and support.

How the overall band works

The four module scores are averaged and the result is rounded to the nearest half or whole band according to IELTS scoring rules. Your target may also include a minimum for each individual module.

Overall score is not the whole story

A programme might ask for an overall 6.5 with no component below 6.0. Meeting the overall score alone would not satisfy that example if one module is below the minimum.

06 · Preparation roadmap

An 8-week plan with a purpose.

Adapt the weekly workload to your starting level and deadline. The key is a repeatable cycle: practise, check answers, diagnose the error and practise the weak point again.

WEEK 1 · DIAGNOSE

Find your baseline

Complete a timed diagnostic or full mock. Record each module score, question types missed and the three highest-impact weaknesses.

WEEKS 2–3 · BUILD

Fix foundations

Practise question types deliberately. Build vocabulary in context, review grammar patterns and listen/read actively rather than passively.

WEEKS 4–5 · PRODUCE

Focus on Writing and Speaking

Write Task 1 and Task 2 responses each week, seek feedback and record Speaking answers. Review structure, relevance and clarity.

WEEKS 6–7 · TIME

Train under exam conditions

Complete timed sections and at least one full mock. Keep an error log and repeat practice for recurring mistakes.

WEEK 8 · REFINE

Polish and prepare

Review common errors, confirm booking and ID requirements, plan your route and protect your sleep before test day.

08 · Module strategies

Small habits, better practice.

🎧

Listening: predict, then verify

Use the question to predict the kind of answer needed. After practice, check the transcript and note whether you missed a synonym, a correction, a plural ending or a spelling detail.

📚

Reading: prove each answer

Skim for structure, scan for names/dates/key terms, then read the relevant lines carefully. For True/False/Not Given, decide only from what the text supports.

✍️

Writing: answer the actual prompt

Plan your position and paragraph purpose before writing. Use examples that support the question, keep the logic clear, and reserve a few minutes to check grammar and word choice.

🗣️

Speaking: develop, don't memorise

Give a direct answer, add a reason and then a concrete example or detail. Practise explaining unfamiliar topics naturally instead of memorising complete scripts.

10 · Avoid common mistakes

Don't lose points to avoidable errors.

01

Booking the wrong test

Verify Academic, General Training or a required UKVI test before paying.

02

Practising without review

A score alone doesn't teach you. Identify why each mistake happened.

03

Ignoring the clock

Practise timing early, not only in the final week.

04

Memorising essays or scripts

Prepared language can become irrelevant or unnatural. Learn structure instead.

05

Forgetting module minimums

Check each skill requirement as well as the overall band.

06

Leaving logistics late

Check ID, venue, result deadline and booking policies in advance.

11 · FAQ

Quick answers.

Many learners plan several weeks of preparation, but the right timeline depends on your current level, target band, study time and feedback access. Start with a diagnostic rather than choosing a timeline blindly.
The skills assessed are intended to be the same. Choose a delivery format based on your comfort with typing, reading on screen and the available local options.
IELTS One Skill Retake is available only under particular conditions and in participating locations. Check current eligibility and confirm that your receiving institution or authority accepts it.
IELTS results are commonly treated as valid for two years, but the receiving institution or authority sets its own acceptance rules. Verify the policy that applies to your application.
Check the official policy before deciding what to do next. Depending on the institution, options may include a retake, an accepted alternative test or an English preparation pathway—but none is guaranteed.
Use the official IELTS website and your test provider for test details, and the official university, professional body or immigration authority for the score requirement that applies to you.
