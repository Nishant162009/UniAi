01 · Start here

What is IELTS, and do you need it?

Know what you are preparing for before you book anything.

What it is

The International English Language Testing System measures English in listening, reading, writing and speaking. It is accepted by universities, employers and immigration authorities in many countries.

Do you need it?

Usually if your previous degree was not taught in English or your nationality is not on the university's exempt list. Some universities accept a medium-of-instruction letter instead, so check first.

Always confirm the exact test type, overall band and minimum per module on your course page. These change by programme.
02 · Test format

Four modules, one band score.

Each module is scored from 0 to 9, then averaged into your overall band.

L

Listening

About 30 minutes plus transfer time, 40 questions across 4 recordings. You hear each recording once.

R

Reading

60 minutes, 40 questions, 3 long texts. Includes matching, True/False/Not Given and summary completion.

W

Writing

60 minutes. Task 1 (about 20 min): describe a graph or write a letter, 150+ words. Task 2 (about 40 min): essay, 250+ words. Task 2 carries more weight.

S

Speaking

11–14 minutes face to face with an examiner, in three parts: introduction, a talk on a topic, and a discussion.

Listening, Reading and Writing are taken the same day. Speaking may be on the same day or within a few days either side.

06 · Prep plan

An 8-week plan you can actually follow.

WEEK 1

Diagnose

Take a full timed mock. Note your band in each module and set a target for each.

WEEKS 2–3

Fix the foundations

Listening: practise note-taking and spelling. Reading: learn each question type. Build 15 new words a day.

WEEKS 4–5

Writing and speaking

Write 2 Task 1 and 2 Task 2 pieces a week and get feedback. Record yourself answering Speaking Part 2.

WEEKS 6–7

Timed practice

Do full mocks under exam conditions. Review every mistake and keep an error log.

WEEK 8

Polish and rest

Light revision, check ID and test centre route, and sleep well before test day.

07 · Module tips

What lifts each band.

L

Listening

Read the questions before the audio. Watch for spelling, plurals and word limits; wrong spelling loses marks.

R

Reading

Do not read every word. Skim for structure, find keywords, and manage your time at about 20 minutes per passage.

W

Writing

Answer every part of the question, structure clearly and use varied but accurate grammar. Do not memorise essays.

S

Speaking

Extend answers with reasons and examples. Natural, fluent speech beats memorised scripts.

08 · Booking

How to register and what to carry.

Booking

Register through an official IELTS test centre (such as British Council or IDP in India), choose paper or computer format, pick a date and pay the fee. Check the current fee on the centre's site.

Test day

Carry the same ID you registered with, arrive early, and leave bags and phones as instructed. Results are usually available within days for computer tests and about 13 days for paper tests.

09 · Avoid these

Mistakes that cost bands.

01

Memorised answers

Examiners recognise scripts and mark them down.

02

Ignoring word counts

Under-length writing loses marks.

03

Skipping timed practice

Knowing English is not the same as finishing on time.

04

Wrong test type

Booking General instead of Academic wastes the fee.

05

Last-minute booking

Popular dates fill up before deadlines.

06

Neglecting Speaking

Many students prepare it last, and it shows.
