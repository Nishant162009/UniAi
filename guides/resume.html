01 · Start here

Your resume is evidence, not a biography.

A strong admissions resume quickly shows what you have built, studied, led or improved. Every line should help the reader understand why you are prepared for the programme.

📄

What admissions wants

Relevant education, projects, research, internships, leadership, skills and measurable outcomes—presented clearly and honestly.

🎯

What to cut

Irrelevant hobbies, outdated skills, long paragraphs, personal details, photo requirements and achievements you cannot explain in an interview.

First rule: If a university provides a template, page limit or required sections, follow it exactly. Otherwise, use a clean one-page resume unless your academic record genuinely requires a CV.
02 · Resume or CV?

Choose the right document.

A resume is a focused summary of relevant experience. A CV is a detailed record of academic work, including research, publications, teaching, awards and presentations.

FeatureResumeAcademic CVUse it when…
PurposeShow relevant skills and impactShow complete academic recordMatch the document to the application request
LengthUsually one page for studentsCan be longerUse a resume unless research output requires a CV
Best forMaster’s, MBA, internships, jobsResearch degrees, academic rolesCheck whether the university asks for “resume” or “CV”
Core sectionsEducation, projects, experience, skillsEducation, research, publications, teaching, awardsInclude only relevant material
ToneConcise and impact-focusedFormal and comprehensiveKeep formatting consistent in both
Step 1Check the requirementDoes it say resume, CV or curriculum vitae?
Step 2Check your profileDo you have research, publications or teaching?
Step 3Choose formatResume for focus; CV for academic depth.
Step 4Order sectionsPut the strongest relevant evidence first.
Step 5Tailor and exportMatch keywords, then save as PDF.
Oxford example: Where a separate CV is required, Oxford says it should usually be one or two pages and summarise recent achievements, qualifications, publications and relevant experience; it also notes that a page is often enough at master’s level.
03 · Structure

Sections that matter.

Use only sections that add relevant evidence. For most students, education and projects should appear near the top; for experienced applicants, work experience may come first.

04 · Writing bullets

Use action, evidence and result.

Start with a strong action verb, describe the specific work, and add a measurable result where truthful. Avoid “I,” “we,” passive phrasing and vague responsibilities.

FormulaWeak bulletStronger bulletWhy it works
Action + task + resultWorked on a website project.Built a responsive college event website using HTML, CSS and JavaScript, increasing registration by 35%.Shows contribution and outcome
Problem + action + impactHelped with data entry.Cleaned 2,000+ survey records in Excel and created validation rules, reducing duplicate entries.Quantifies scale and value
Leadership + outcomeWas part of a team.Led a four-member team to build a campus navigation app; presented the prototype to 120 students.Shows ownership and scale
Research + methodDid research on machine learning.Designed a sentiment-analysis pipeline using Python and scikit-learn; achieved 87% accuracy on a labelled dataset.Shows method and result
Weak verbStronger alternativesUse when…
ParticipatedContributed to, collaborated on, supportedYou had a defined but supporting role
Worked onBuilt, developed, implemented, analysedYou created or improved something
HelpedStreamlined, coordinated, improved, resolvedYou made a measurable contribution
Responsible forManaged, led, owned, deliveredYou had direct accountability
Honesty rule: Quantify only what you can verify. “Improved accuracy by 12%” is powerful only if you can explain the dataset, baseline and method.

06 · ATS and formatting

Make it easy to scan.

Many portals and employers use applicant tracking systems. A simple, text-based PDF with clear headings is usually safer than a heavily designed graphic resume.

ElementRecommendedAvoidWhy
File formatPDF unless the portal requires WordJPG, PNG or password-protected filesPreserves layout and is widely readable
FontCalibri, Arial, Helvetica or InterDecorative or very small fontsImproves readability
LayoutClear headings, bullets and white spaceTables, text boxes and complex columnsReduces parsing problems
LengthOne page for most studentsTwo pages of repeated contentKeeps the strongest evidence visible
KeywordsUse terms from the programme or job descriptionKeyword stuffingImproves relevance without misleading readers
Formatting guidance: MIT recommends a conservative font no smaller than 10pt, at least half-inch margins, one page for most applicants, action-verb bullets and quantified results where possible. [119][123]
07 · Myths vs truths

Stop believing these resume myths.

MythTruthWhat to do instead
“A colourful template looks professional.”Clarity matters more than decoration.Use a clean, readable layout with consistent formatting.
“Longer resumes show more experience.”Irrelevant length hides your best evidence.Keep one page unless you have substantial relevant experience.
“Every job needs an objective.”Most modern resumes do not need one.Use a targeted summary only if it adds value.
“A photo makes it personal.”Photos are usually unnecessary and may be inappropriate in some markets.Follow the country and employer/university norm.
“I should list every skill.”Irrelevant skills distract from your fit.List skills you can demonstrate or discuss confidently.
“AI can write my whole resume.”AI can help polish language, but it cannot know your real impact.Use AI to critique clarity and grammar after you provide accurate content.

Your resume workflow

STEP 1

Gather achievements

List projects, internships, competitions, leadership roles, tools and measurable results.

STEP 2

Choose the format

Use a resume for most applications; use a CV when academic depth is required.

STEP 3

Prioritise relevance

Put your strongest, most relevant evidence near the top.

STEP 4

Write impact bullets

Use action verb + task + measurable result.

STEP 5

Tailor keywords

Mirror important terms from the course or job description—truthfully.

STEP 6

Proofread

Check spelling, dates, grammar, consistency and contact details.

STEP 7

Get feedback

Ask a mentor whether your strongest achievement is immediately visible.

STEP 8

Export and submit

Save as PDF with a professional file name, unless another format is required.
