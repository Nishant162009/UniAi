01 · Start here

Know whether GMAT fits your goal.

The GMAT Focus Edition is designed for graduate management admissions, especially MBA and specialised master’s programmes. Many schools also accept the GRE, so your first task is to confirm which test your target programmes prefer.

💼

Who takes it

Applicants to MBA, MiM, finance, management, analytics and some other business-related programmes. Requirements differ by school, degree and scholarship route.

🎯

What to verify first

Check whether GMAT or GRE is accepted, whether there is a minimum score, what the recent class profile looks like, and whether scholarship deadlines require an earlier test date.

Important: The GMAT Focus Edition has no Analytical Writing Assessment. Instead, Data Insights is a full scored section and contributes equally to your total score.
02 · Test format

03 · Scoring

205–805, with equal section weight.

The GMAT Focus Edition total score ranges from 205 to 805 in 10-point increments. All three sections are weighted equally toward the total, and each section is reported on a 60–90 scale.

ScoreRangeIncrementsWhat it shows
Total score205–80510 pointsCombined performance across all three sections
Quantitative Reasoning60–901 pointProblem-solving performance
Verbal Reasoning60–901 pointCritical reasoning and reading performance
Data Insights60–901 pointData interpretation and reasoning performance

There is no writing score

Unlike the previous GMAT, the Focus Edition does not include an Analytical Writing Assessment. Your score report focuses on Quant, Verbal and Data Insights.

Balance matters

Because all three sections count equally, a very strong Quant score cannot fully compensate for a weak Data Insights or Verbal performance.

Validity: GMAT Focus scores are valid for five years, but each school sets its own application and score-submission policy.

05 · Preparation roadmap

An 8-week plan with a purpose.

Use a diagnostic to locate your biggest gap, then follow a repeatable cycle: practise timed questions, review every error, identify the cause, and drill that weakness again.

WEEK 1 · DIAGNOSE

Find your baseline

Take an official-style GMAT Focus mock. Record section scores, pacing, accuracy and recurring error types.

WEEKS 2–3 · FOUNDATIONS

Rebuild Quant and Verbal

Review arithmetic, algebra, fractions, percentages and word-problem setup. Practise critical reasoning and reading comprehension deliberately.

WEEKS 4–5 · DATA INSIGHTS

Master data reasoning

Drill Data Sufficiency, tables, graphs, two-part analysis and multi-source reasoning. Learn to extract only the data needed.

WEEKS 6–7 · SIMULATE

Train under exam conditions

Take full-length mocks with your chosen section order. Review pacing, careless errors and question-type patterns after each test.

WEEK 8 · REFINE

Polish and prepare

Do light review, revisit your error log, confirm booking details and protect sleep before test day.

06 · Section strategy

Small habits, better scores.

🧮

Quant: reason before calculating

Look for number properties, estimation, divisibility and strategic substitution. Avoid treating every question as a full algebra problem.

🧠

Verbal: find the argument

For Critical Reasoning, identify the conclusion and supporting evidence before evaluating choices. For Reading Comprehension, track the author’s purpose and structure.

📊

Data Insights: scan before solving

Read the prompt first, then scan table headers, graph axes, units and trends. In Data Sufficiency, test one statement at a time.

⏱️

Pacing: protect accuracy

Use the bookmark-and-review feature strategically, but do not save too many questions for the end. A steady pace protects both accuracy and confidence.
